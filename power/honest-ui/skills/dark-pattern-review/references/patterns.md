## Visual interference

- **What it is:** The choice the company wants is big and bright; the one you may want is faded, tiny or hidden in plain sight. Nothing is missing, it’s just made hard to see.
- **Where you meet it:** cookie banners, cancel screens, upgrade prompts, unsubscribe pages.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** the accept control is a big filled button and the refuse control a small, pale link or missing from the first layer; different size, colour weight or placement for two equal choices
- **US:** The FTC report lists a bold "Accept" beside a greyed-out, small "Reject" as subverting privacy choices (Appendix A, "Subverting Privacy Preferences"; section IV). In California, agreement obtained through a dark pattern is not consent (CCPA as amended by the CPRA, Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(h)). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Consent to non-essential cookies must be freely given (ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3) with the GDPR’s consent rules); EU data protection authorities said a banner must not clearly push you to accept, and that a "reject" button too faded to read can be misleading (EDPB cookie banner taskforce report, January 2023). Source: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-01/edpb_20230118_report_cookie_banner_taskforce_en.pdf
- **Tip:** Find the quiet option before you click the loud one; "Reject all" is often there, just pale.
- **In the game:** The Foyer

## Trick wording

- **What it is:** The sentence is built to confuse: double negatives, flipped checkboxes, a "No" that means yes. Skim it and you agree to something you never wanted.
- **Where you meet it:** sign-up forms, email preferences, cancel flows ("No, cancel" that doesn’t cancel).
- **Signs to look for in a page:** a checkbox or toggle label with two negations, or wording where ticking means opting out
- **US:** The FTC report lists "trick questions", with the example "Uncheck the box if you prefer not to receive email updates" (Appendix A). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** A request for consent must be in clear and plain language (GDPR Art. 7(2)); silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity are not consent (GDPR Recital 32). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj
- **Tip:** Read checkbox sentences twice, slowly, and ask: if I leave this alone, what happens?
- **In the game:** The Library

## Confirmshaming

- **What it is:** The "no" button is written to make you feel foolish or guilty for saying no. A simple choice becomes a small insult.
- **Where you meet it:** discount and newsletter pop-ups, unsubscribe pages, "Are you sure?" screens.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** a decline control whose words shame the user ("No thanks, I don't like saving")
- **US:** The FTC report lists "confirm shaming", with the example "No, I don’t want to save money" (Appendix A). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** The Digital Services Act bars online platforms from interfaces that deceive or manipulate users or materially distort their free and informed choices (Art. 25(1)), unless the unfair-practices law or the GDPR already covers the practice (Art. 25(2)); Recital 67 mentions presenting choices in a non-neutral manner. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj
- **Tip:** A guilt trip is a sales tactic, not a fact. Click the plain "no"; the page will survive.
- **In the game:** The Dining Room

## Hidden subscription

- **What it is:** "Free" quietly turns into a monthly charge, and the price, the date and the way out sit in small grey print. Many people only find out from their bank statement.
- **Where you meet it:** free trials for streaming, apps and software; "first box free" offers.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** a "free" trial whose renewal price, start date or cancel path sits below the fold, in small print or after the button
- **US:** The FTC report lists "hidden subscription or forced continuity" (Appendix A) and treats free trials that roll into charges as leading to unauthorized charges (section III). The Restore Online Shoppers’ Confidence Act (ROSCA) requires clear disclosure of the material terms before billing details are taken, express informed consent, and a simple way to stop recurring charges (15 U.S.C. § 8403). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Just before you order, the trader must show the total price, the contract’s length and how to end it clearly and prominently, and the order button must say that ordering means paying (Consumer Rights Directive, Art. 8(2)). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj
- **Tip:** Before you enter a card for a free trial, find three things: the price after, the date it starts, the way to cancel. Then set a reminder.
- **In the game:** The Parlour

## Fake urgency

- **What it is:** A countdown or "ends tonight" banner pushes you to decide now, but the deadline is invented and resets when it runs out. Hurry shrinks the time you spend comparing.
- **Where you meet it:** ticket sites, flash sales, travel booking, online courses.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** a countdown near selling words that resets on reload or has no stated end
- **US:** The FTC report lists the "baseless countdown timer": a fake clock "that just goes away or resets when it times out" (Appendix A; section I, false beliefs). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Falsely saying a product or offer is available only for a very limited time, to force a quick decision, is unfair in all circumstances (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Annex I, point 7). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj
- **Tip:** Reload or come back later: a real deadline doesn’t move. If the clock resets, so can you.
- **In the game:** The Conservatory

## Fake scarcity

- **What it is:** "Only 1 left!" sits on every size, every time, whatever the real stock. The fear of missing out does the selling.
- **Where you meet it:** clothing shops, hotel and flight sites, marketplaces.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** "only N left" or "almost gone" on many items at once, or with no stock source
- **US:** The FTC report lists the "false low stock message", with the example "Only 1 left in stock – order soon" (Appendix A; section I: claims "that an item is almost sold out when there is actually ample supply"). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Misleading people about a product’s availability is a misleading commercial practice (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Art. 6(1)(b)). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj
- **Tip:** If every option is "almost gone", none of them is. Pick what fits, not what’s "last".
- **In the game:** The Ballroom

## Fake social proof

- **What it is:** "38 people are looking at this room" and glowing reviews no guest ever wrote make a choice feel popular and safe. The crowd is made up.
- **Where you meet it:** hotel and travel sites, shops with "someone just bought this" pop-ups, review sections.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** "N people are viewing" counters or unverified, undated reviews
- **US:** The FTC report lists "false activity messages", with the example "24 other people are viewing this listing" (Appendix A; section I). An FTC rule in force since October 2024 bans fake consumer reviews and testimonials and fake social-media indicators (16 CFR Part 465, in force 21 October 2024). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Submitting or commissioning fake reviews, or claiming reviews come from real customers without checking, is unfair in all circumstances (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Annex I, points 23b and 23c). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj
- **Tip:** Trust dated reviews tied to real stays or purchases; ignore "people are looking" counters.
- **In the game:** The Portrait Gallery

## Sneaking

- **What it is:** Something you never chose ("Ghost Protection £2.99") slips into your basket and comes back when you take it out. You pay for it unless you notice.
- **Where you meet it:** checkout pages, ticket and travel booking, insurance and warranty add-ons, donation add-ons.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** an item, insurance or add-on placed in the basket without the user's action, or one that comes back after removal
- **US:** The FTC report lists "sneak-into-basket": "Automatically adding items to the shopping cart without a shopper’s permission" (Appendix A). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** A trader needs your express consent for any extra payment; if it was inferred from a default you had to reject, you’re entitled to a refund (Consumer Rights Directive, Art. 22). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj
- **Tip:** Read the basket line by line before you pay; anything you didn’t add is a ghost.
- **In the game:** The Pantry

## Hidden costs

- **What it is:** The first price you see ("From £1/month") isn’t what you pay; fees appear only at the last step. By then you’ve spent time and feel committed.
- **Where you meet it:** ticketing, food delivery, hotels ("resort fees"), subscriptions, rentals.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** fees that appear only at the last step; "from" prices instead of totals
- **US:** The FTC report calls it "drip pricing": advertising part of the price and adding mandatory charges late, such as a "convenience fee" that appears only at checkout (Appendix A; section II). Since May 2025 an FTC rule requires the total price up front for live-event tickets and short-term lodging (16 CFR Part 464, effective 12 May 2025). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Before you’re bound, the trader must tell you the total price including taxes and all extra charges (Consumer Rights Directive, Art. 6(1)(e)). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj
- **Tip:** Go to the final step before you decide; compare totals, not "from" prices.
- **In the game:** The Study

## Nagging

- **What it is:** The same request pops up again and again until you give in, and "No" only means "not now". You pay with your attention until you say yes.
- **Where you meet it:** app rating prompts, notification and location requests, cookie re-prompts, upgrade pop-ups in games.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** the same prompt again after "Not now", with no way to stop it
- **US:** The FTC report lists "nagging": asking repeatedly, or offering "Yes" and "Not Now" instead of "Yes" and "No" (Appendix A). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** The Digital Services Act names "repeatedly requesting" a choice already made, "especially by presenting pop-ups", as a practice the Commission may issue guidelines on for online platforms (Art. 25(3)(b)). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj
- **Tip:** Look for a real "No" or "Don’t ask again", and turn the prompts off in the app’s settings.
- **In the game:** The Nursery

## Preselection

- **What it is:** Extras are ticked before you arrive: travel insurance, priority boarding, a "lucky charm". If you don’t untick them, you pay.
- **Where you meet it:** flight and travel booking, software installers, newsletter boxes, privacy settings.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** boxes for marketing, sharing, insurance or donations already ticked
- **US:** The FTC report lists "preselection": a default good for the company, not the user, such as trip insurance "automatically tacked on to a purchase unless the customer notices and opts out" (Appendix A). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** Airlines must show optional extras clearly at the start of booking and add them only on an opt-in basis (Regulation (EC) 1008/2008, Art. 23(1)); a default you must reject is not consent to an extra payment (Consumer Rights Directive, Art. 22). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2008/1008/oj
- **Tip:** Scan every ticked box before you pay. A tick you didn’t make is a choice someone made for you.
- **In the game:** The Attic

## Hard to cancel

- **What it is:** Joining takes one click; leaving takes seven pages, a phone call at 3 a.m. or a chat that keeps offering deals. The maze is the point.
- **Where you meet it:** gyms, newspapers, streaming, software subscriptions, memberships.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** signing up takes one step but cancelling needs many, a phone call or a hidden link
- **US:** The FTC report lists "roadblocks to cancellation": easy to sign up, hard to cancel, e.g. a phone line open only at limited hours (Appendix A; section III). ROSCA requires a simple way to stop recurring charges (15 U.S.C. § 8403(3)). The FTC’s 2024 "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025 (Custom Communications v. FTC, No. 24-3137), so the game never rests on it. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf
- **EU:** The Digital Services Act names "making the procedure for terminating a service more difficult than subscribing to it" as a practice the Commission may issue guidelines on for online platforms (Art. 25(3)(c)). Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj
- **Tip:** Before you subscribe, find the cancel path. Cancel in writing if you can and keep the confirmation.
- **In the game:** The Cellar

## Disguised ads

- **What it is:** An advert dressed as something else: a news story, a review, a neutral ranking or a search result. You trust it as independent advice, but someone paid for it.
- **Where you meet it:** "Top 10" lists, sponsored search results, recommended articles and posts that say "partner" only in small print.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** a ranked list or "editor's pick" whose first item is paid, with only a small "partner" or "sponsored" tag in the same style as the rest; search results with no visible ad label.
- **US:** The FTC treats ads that people cannot recognise as ads as deceptive when they make people believe the message is independent or impartial. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/896923/151222deceptiveenforcement.pdf
- **EU:** EU law bans paid editorial content that hides the payment, and search results that hide paid placement or paid ranking; online platforms must mark every ad clearly. Sources: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj (Annex I points 11 and 11a) · https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj (Art. 26(1)(a))
- **Tip:** Look for "Ad", "Sponsored" or "Partner" before you trust a ranking. If a list never says how it ranks, treat its number one as an ad.
- **In the game:** The Map Room (planned)

## Forced action

- **What it is:** You must do something you did not come for, such as create an account, share your contacts or install an app, before you can finish a simple task.
- **Where you meet it:** Downloads behind sign-up walls, checkouts with no guest option, "allow contacts to continue" screens in apps and games.
- **Signs to look for in a page:** a gate between the user and a simple task that asks for an account, contacts or an app install, with no "continue as guest" or "skip".
- **US:** For children under 13, the US COPPA Rule forbids making a game, a prize or another activity depend on the child giving more personal information than the activity reasonably needs. Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.7
- **EU:** Under the GDPR, consent is presumed not freely given when a service is made to depend on agreeing to data use it does not need. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj (Art. 7(4), Recital 43)
- **Tip:** Look for "Continue as guest", "Skip" or "Not now". If a task asks for data it does not need, stop and find another way.
- **In the game:** The Music Room (planned)

## Four more to watch for (definitions only; no legal line has been checked for these yet)

- **Obstruction:** the path you want is made long and tiring on purpose.
- **Comparison prevention:** bundles, odd units or per-week prices that make prices hard to compare.
- **Addictive design:** endless feeds, autoplay and streaks that keep you there.
- **Currency confusion:** gems and coins that hide the real price.
